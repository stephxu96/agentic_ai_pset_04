How do we keep a retiring estimator's quoting knowledge?
by Aidan Vrobel
Oct 15, 2026
Short answer:
Your senior estimator's knowledge isn't written down anywhere. It's judgment: he's the only one who can run your quoting process, he knows what every customer's drawings leave out, he knows which parts are good and which work together, he knows which vendor to use for every part, and he can look at a drawing and know the labor. To keep it, move that judgment into one system the whole team uses, and have newer estimators quote alongside him before he leaves. What actually walks out the door
In a lot of wire harness and cable assembly shops, quoting runs through one person who has done it for 20 or 30 years. When he retires, here's what goes with him:
The process itself.
He's the only one who really knows how to run your quoting process, whether that's a spreadsheet he built, your ERP, or a stack of paper. Nobody else can produce a quote the same way.
The customers.
He knows every customer and exactly what their drawings don't define: the terminals, seals, cavity plugs and coverings they always leave off, and the mistakes they always make.
The parts.
He can look at a part and know whether it's a good part or a bad one, and which parts work together and which don't. That's what keeps the wrong parts from reaching the manufacturing floor.
The vendors.
For every part, he knows which vendor to go to.
Your internal part names.
He knows what parts are called in your ERP, which often isn't the manufacturer part number. One manufacturer we spoke with had a customer that sent everything in its own internal part numbers. The estimator knew every cross-reference, never put them into a system, and when he left, they left with him.
A feel for labor.
Most veterans don't count every operation. They look at a drawing and just know how long it will take. That's the hardest thing to hand over, because it was never written down in the first place.
How to keep it
1. Get the process out of one person's head
If only one person can run the quoting process, that's the first risk to fix. Move quoting into one system the whole team uses, so the process doesn't depend on his spreadsheet or his memory of how the ERP works.
2. Capture what each customer leaves out
Sit with him on real RFQs from your top customers and write down what each one's drawings never define. Turn those into checks that run on every quote, not notes in a binder.
3. Capture his parts and vendor knowledge
Record which parts work together, which ones he avoids, which vendor he uses for what, and what each part is called in your ERP. That's the knowledge that keeps quotes buildable and correctly priced.
4. Turn his feel for labor into a standard
Have him price a set of recent jobs against one standard labor template and compare the template to his gut number. Where they differ, adjust the template. When he leaves, the template carries his judgment instead of his memory.
5. Overlap before he leaves
Have newer estimators quote real jobs while he reviews their work. Every correction he makes is knowledge you haven't captured yet.

FAQ
What quoting knowledge do we lose when a senior estimator retires?
Mostly judgment: how to run your quoting process, what each customer's drawings leave out, which parts are good and work together, which vendor to use for each part, what parts are called in your ERP, and a feel for how much labor a drawing takes.
Our estimator prices labor by feel. How do we capture that?
Have him price recent jobs against one standard labor template and compare the template to his gut number. Adjust the template where they differ, so it carries his judgment after he leaves.
How long before he retires should we start?
As early as possible. Capturing his knowledge takes real quotes and real time with him, and every correction he makes during overlap is knowledge you'd otherwise lose.
Can a newer estimator really quote like a 30-year veteran?
Not from memory. But if the veteran's customer, parts, vendor and labor knowledge lives in a system that applies it to every quote, a newer estimator starts from the same place and handles the judgment calls.
