How do we stop losing money on cable assembly quotes?
by Aidan Vrobel
Oct 15, 2026
Short answer:
Most money lost on cable assembly and wire harness quotes comes from four places. First, what the customer's drawing doesn't define: terminals, seals, cavity plugs, backshells, wire lengths and coverings that only surface on the manufacturing floor. Second, pricing that never gets compared across the whole supply chain. Third, labor that two estimators count two different ways. Fourth, plain data-entry mistakes: typos, wrong part numbers, missed zeros and misplaced decimal points. Fix them in that order.
Key facts
The costliest quoting mistakes are usually engineering gaps in the customer's drawing, not math errors.

1. What the customer's drawing doesn't define
This is the biggest leak, and it's an engineering problem, not an estimating one. Customer drawings routinely leave out information the manufacturing floor needs to build the harness:
Cavity plugs, terminals, wire seals and backshells
The wrong wire, or wire that doesn't match the connector
Wire lengths, which are hard to calculate when the drawing doesn't state them
Coverings, or the wrong coverings for the application
Labels and other build details that "everyone knows" but nobody wrote down
Sometimes the customer simply made a mistake on the drawing. Either way, under turnaround pressure nobody catches it at quote time. It shows up on the manufacturing floor, after you've bought the wrong parts or parts that don't work together. By then the price is locked and the margin is gone.
How to fix it:
review every drawing for what's missing, not just what's listed. Check that every connector has its terminals, seals and plugs, that the wire matches the connector, and that lengths and coverings are defined. Send the customer questions before you price, not after you win.
2. Pricing nobody can fully compare
Stale pricing is part of the problem: last month's spreadsheet price is not today's negotiated rate. But the bigger issue is that most shops never look at the whole supply chain. Checking 20 vendors for every part, across 5 price breaks, across 10 assemblies is thousands of combinations. A person can't do that, so they check one or two favorite distributors and move on, and the best price and availability get missed.
How to fix it:
price every quote on the day you send it, at your own negotiated rates, across every distributor you buy from and every quantity break the customer asked for.
3. Labor counted two different ways
Give the same harness to two estimators and you'll often get two labor numbers. One counts every termination, the other estimates by feel. One remembers the overmold, the other doesn't. The quote that wins is often the one that undercounted.
How to fix it:
put everyone on one standard labor template and count operations against it the same way on every quote.
4. Data-entry mistakes
Every manual quote means retyping: part numbers from the drawing into a spreadsheet, prices from a distributor website, quantities across every price break. People make typos. A transcription error, a wrong part number, a missed zero or a misplaced decimal point can mean buying the wrong part or pricing a line at a tenth of what it costs. These aren't rare. They happen all the time, and they come straight out of margin.
How to fix it:
retype as little as possible. Pull part numbers, quantities and prices straight from the source instead of copying them by hand, and double-check any line that looks out of range before the quote goes out.

FAQ
What's the most common reason cable assembly quotes lose money?
Information the customer's drawing doesn't define, such as terminals, seals, cavity plugs, backshells, wire lengths and coverings. It usually isn't caught until the manufacturing floor, after parts are bought.
How often should I re-price a quote?
On the day you send it. Distributor prices and availability move constantly, so a price from last month's spreadsheet is a guess.
Why can't my estimators just check more distributors?
Because the combinations multiply fast. Twenty vendors, five price breaks and ten assemblies is thousands of price points per RFQ. Software can check them all; a person can't.
Can I keep my own labor template?
Yes. Cableteque loads your existing template as-is and fills 60–80% of it automatically, so every estimator counts labor the same way.
