Step 1: Capture The Drawing Package
The first job is to get the customer drawing into the system in a form it can read and parse. Cableteque says its platform accepts PDF and other common file types, which lets quoting teams start from the documents OEMs already send instead of rebuilding the package elsewhere first.
This step matters because bad intake creates bad output. If the drawing is incomplete, unreadable, or split across multiple files, the estimator spends time chasing data instead of pricing the job. A clean upload is the point where the clock starts and where the rest of the workflow becomes measurable.
Upload the customer PDF drawing, BOM sheet, and any supporting notes as a single quote package so the system has the full context before extraction begins.
Verify that the file set includes the latest revision, because the value of automatic extraction drops fast if the team starts from an old print.
Confirm the package is ready when the drawing preview opens cleanly and the project file is available for extraction and review.
Step 2: Extract The BOM From The PDF
This is the step that answers the core question directly. Cableteque says it can automatically extract BOM items, descriptions, and customer part numbers from OEM PDFs, turning a manual recreation task into a machine-assisted import.
The company also claims this shrinks BOM recreation time from 30 to 45 minutes down to about 2 minutes, with up to 96% time savings on rebuilding BOMs from customer drawings and PDFs. In a quoting department that handles dozens of RFQs each week, that difference changes how many jobs get touched before the deadline. Cableteque's own blog post,
Spent Years Extracting BOMs by Hand. Then We Built a Better Way.
, describes the same workflow in plain language.
Start the BOM extraction after the file preview loads so the system can read tables, part callouts, and text blocks from the drawing itself.
Check the extracted line items against the source PDF to confirm the system has captured the right descriptions, customer part numbers, and quantities.
Mark this step complete when the BOM is visible in structured form and no major line items remain trapped in the source drawing.
Step 3: Normalize Part Data And Customer Part Numbers
Once the BOM is extracted, the next task is to turn messy customer language into usable manufacturing data. Cableteque says it can map descriptive inputs such as "blk tape" to black Tesa 3/4 inch tape, apply conversion rules like loose piece terminal to reel, and store customer part numbers while mapping them to MPNs.
That is where a lot of quoting teams lose time. A part description from an OEM print often does not match the supplier catalog, and one estimator may know the cross-reference from memory while another has to hunt for it. Cableteque's own materials say the platform applies that tribal knowledge consistently, which cuts rework and keeps the BOM in one naming system.
Review each extracted line for customer shorthand, internal aliases, and part conversion rules so the platform can resolve them to a standard component record.
Add approved alternates and equivalency rules where your team already has sourcing preferences, because those choices affect both price and lead time.
Finish this step when each critical item has a normalized description, customer part number, and mapped MPN or approved alternate.
Step 4: Check Design Gaps And Sourcing Logic
The BOM is only useful if the design can actually be built. Cableteque says its AI-powered analysis flags missing information or errors early, and its sourcing logic can autopick missing terminals, cavity plugs, and seals from connector compatibility and wire gauge data.
That is a major shift from the old process, where an estimator often discovers the gap after hours of manual comparison. In Cableteque's WHMA survey of 42 manufacturers, 57.14% said BOM or design completeness was a top challenge, and 73.81% described quoting as manual and time-intensive. Those numbers explain why early validation pays off so quickly.
Run the design check after normalization so the software can compare the extracted BOM against connector rules, wire gauge, and missing accessory logic.
Review any flagged gaps before moving to pricing, since missing seals, cavity plugs, or terminals can distort both cost and feasibility.
Close this step when the BOM has no unresolved design gaps that would block sourcing or labor estimation.
Why the sourcing pass matters
This step keeps the quote tied to real parts, not guesswork, and it reduces the back-and-forth that usually eats an entire day.
- Cableteque says its parts database includes more than 2,000,000 components, which gives sourcing a broader starting point than a spreadsheet search.
- The platform also claims real-time integration with supplier databases, so availability and price can update during the quote instead of after it.
- The result is a cleaner handoff to labor and margin review, with fewer surprises at approval time.
Step 5: Build Labor And Cost Inputs
Now the team can estimate labor with the BOM already cleaned up. Cableteque says it generates time estimates from historical data and applies dynamic cost calculation using real-time inputs, which means the quote reflects both material and labor in one pass.
The practical payoff is speed and consistency. The company says the larger quoting cycle can shrink from 7 to 10 days to about 30 minutes in many pilots, and its broader performance claims include reduced manual input by up to 96%. Cableteque also says the platform reduces the need for deep manual engineering intervention on every quote, which frees senior staff for the jobs that actually need their judgment.
Load the extracted BOM into the labor estimation workflow so the software can assign task counts and estimated time by operation.
Check the material pricing against supplier data and your approved source preferences before applying overhead and margin.
Confirm the estimate is complete when labor, material, and quote totals are all visible in the same review view.
Step 6: Review, Approve, And Send The Quote
The final step is internal review, where the platform turns structured BOM data into a customer-ready quote. Cableteque says one-click reviews and approval help teams move quickly, while compliance checks and crosslist validation catch incorrect part numbers on the first pass.
This is the point where slow quoting turns into lost business. When a competitor replies in a day and your team replies in a week, the customer often stops waiting. Cableteque's own content on
reducing quoting errors and delays
frames that delay as a margin problem, not just an efficiency problem.
Review the quote package for part accuracy, labor assumptions, supplier availability, and any open compliance flags before final sign-off.
Send the approved quote in the customer's required format so the response goes out without extra rework.
Treat the step as complete only when the quote is approved internally and ready for customer delivery.

That shift matters for product managers, engineering managers, and operations managers because it changes capacity without adding headcount. Cableteque says one outcome is a quote turnaround drop from 7 to 10 days to about 30 minutes, which is a large enough gap to affect win rate, estimator workload, and customer response time at the same time. The workflow is also non-disruptive, which means teams can add it without rebuilding every existing process on day one.
The quoting department becomes less reactive. Senior engineers spend less time on manual data entry and more time on the jobs that need engineering judgment, while operations gets a cleaner path from RFQ to approval.
