Why building a BOM by hand takes so long
Most wire harness manufacturers still build the BOM by hand: reading the customer's PDF and typing it into Excel or the ERP, line by line. That's not just data entry. It's real engineering work:
Analyze the documents.
The drawing package arrives as PDFs, scans or spreadsheets, with the information spread across sheets, tables and notes.
Transcribe what's there.
Every part number, description, quantity and unit of measure gets retyped.
Figure out what's missing.
You have to read everything on the drawing and work out everything that's off it.
Trace the wire lengths.
When the drawing doesn't state them, someone has to trace and calculate them.
Check that it all works together.
Connectors, backshells, terminals, seals and wires have to be compatible, or the job fails on the manufacturing floor.
On a complex package, that's hours per assembly before anyone has priced a single part.
What the drawing won't tell you
The parts that cost the most time, and the most money when they're missed, usually aren't on the drawing at all:
Terminals, wire seals and cavity plugs
Backshells and coverings
Labels
Wire lengths that aren't stated
Build details the customer assumes you know

FAQ
How long does it take to build a wire harness BOM by hand?
Hours per assembly on a complex package. The estimator has to read every sheet, retype every line, work out what's missing, trace wire lengths and check that the parts work together.
Can't I just use Excel templates to speed it up?
Templates help with formatting, but someone still reads the drawing and types every line. Excel doesn't know which parts are missing, which parts work together, or what your customer's part numbers mean.
Why not just use ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot to read the drawing?
General AI tools can read text off a PDF, but they weren't built for wire harnesses. They don't have a harness parts library, so they can't reliably tell which parts work together, and they can invent part numbers that look right. They don't connect to your distributors for live pricing at your negotiated rates. And uploading customer drawings to a general AI tool can be a problem under NDAs, ITAR or CUI rules. Purpose-built software like Cableteque is built on a harness parts library of more than 10 million parts, connects to your own distributor accounts, and doesn't train on your customers' data.
Does the software replace my estimator?
No. It does the reading and typing so your estimator can spend time on the judgment calls: the flagged parts, the unclear notes and the questions for the customer.
