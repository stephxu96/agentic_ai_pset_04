# What Agentic CAD’s Checks Actually Prove

**Question:** What do current checks prove about AI-driven CAD? **Answer:** They support checked first drafts for bounded geometry tasks. They do not establish that generated CAD is engineering-approved, manufacturable, or ready for physical use.

*As of October 16, 2026.*

Watch the video version: [VIDEO LINK]

Engineering.com tested Backflip’s drawing-to-CAD output. A practitioner imported two STEP files into Autodesk Fusion, assigned the specified material, and checked each part’s mass against the drawing criterion. Both passed that test, according to the report. [02-c1]

This checks the mass of two imported solids with an assigned material. It matches the competition’s mass criterion, but does not check tolerances, design intent, manufacturing constraints, or the native editable model.

Mass can be a useful rejection test: a part with the wrong mass under the specified material has failed a stated requirement. A match, however, cannot show that every dimension is right. Different shapes can have the same volume and mass. A team using this workflow would need to inspect the dimensions and features that govern the part’s intended use.

The distinction matters. Backflip’s Fusion add-in imported only part of one tested model. On a seven-body wire-shelf drawing, it created one body. [02-c2] These failures do not mean every import fails. They show that passing a STEP mass test does not establish reliable editable-CAD or multibody output.

NVIDIA’s vendor demonstration separates visual checks from checks of joints, collision geometry, and physical properties, then tests grasps in simulation. [02-c3] Each tests a different claim. Visual comparison addresses scale and appearance. Physics checks address configured simulation properties. A simulated grasp shows success only under the selected simulation conditions.

None proves that an asset will work on physical hardware or that a CAD design meets manufacturing tolerances. Read together, the Backflip test and NVIDIA demonstration suggest a rule: specify the intended use, retain editable files, and test the property that matters. A valid import, convincing image, or simulated grasp cannot substitute for other checks.

That rule also helps assign responsibility. The tool may generate a candidate and record its checks, but the engineering team must decide which failures can be screened automatically and which require review. For example, a body-count check can flag a missing component; it cannot decide whether the remaining geometry reflects the designer’s intent. The output should remain a proposal until the responsible reviewer accepts the relevant checks.

The MIT Computational Design and Fabrication Group survey likewise says model outputs can serve as first drafts, while tolerances, structural behavior, manufacturability, and safe unsupervised operation need more evaluation. [02-c4] This supports a first-draft role, not approval for engineering use.

The Physical AI Podcast reports that five models each ran once per RobotWorld task and left 63 tasks unsolved by all models. [02-c5] The supplied transcript has not been checked against the audio. One run per task is sensitive to chance, and simulation counts do not establish real-robot reliability.

The evidence remains thin. Backflip’s test used three TooTallToby drawings, with no reported repeated trials; the author says they may be simpler than shop drawings. NVIDIA’s vendor demonstration has no independent physical-transfer replication. Neither source establishes rates for meeting production tolerances, preserving editable features, handling assemblies, or producing manufacturable designs.

Treat AI-driven CAD as a first-draft tool with a test plan. Retain the artifact and check the relevant property: mass or dimensions for geometry, feature-tree integrity for editable CAD, joint and collision behavior for simulation, and physical trials for deployment. Seek repeated tests on production-like drawings, public scoring methods, disclosed model and tool settings, and independent hardware tests.
