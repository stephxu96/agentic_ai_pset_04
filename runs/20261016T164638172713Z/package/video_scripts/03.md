## Scene 1 — Answer first

**Narration:** What should engineering leaders do with the latest AI-assisted CAD, simulation, and PLM workflows? Pilot them first where they help people find, prepare, or review artifacts. Do not give them unchecked authority over an engineering system of record. As of October 16, 2026, that is the practical sequence supported by these sources. ## Scene 2 — Start with discovery

**Narration:** Onshape says its AI-Generated Thumbnail Description Search is an opt-in Onshape Labs feature. It says the released feature applies to public documents only. The stated check is narrow: retrieval from model thumbnails. A user can search visible characteristics, then inspect a candidate artifact.

That check supports a discovery claim. It does not establish geometric correctness, part equivalence, manufacturability, or suitability for reuse. A retrieved bracket may look right without having the right dimensions, material, tolerances, or revision state.

**Visual:** Split screen. Left: thumbnail search for “red bracket.” Right: a checklist marked “Not checked”: dimensions, material, tolerance, revision. Label: “Observed check: retrieve public-document candidates from thumbnails.”

## Scene 3 — Simulation preparation has stronger checks

**Narration:** NVIDIA describes a more involved boundary: preparing a robot asset for simulation. Its walkthrough says the workflow checks imported robot scale, parts, and appearance against references. It says the workflow configures joints, collision geometry, and physical properties. Then it reruns simulation-ready checks and a grasp test after fixing issues.

Those are source claims, and the final sequence is tested in Isaac Sim. The observed checks match readiness for the demonstrated simulated workflow. They do not, by themselves, show that a real robot will perform reliably in a physical environment.

**Visual:** Labeled pipeline: “References → scale, parts, appearance check → joints, collision geometry, physical properties → rerun checks + grasp test → Isaac Sim.” A boundary line below: “Checked in simulation, not physical deployment.”

**Source on screen:** 03-c3, 03-c4, 03-c5, 03-c6 — NVIDIA, “5 Steps to Create SimReady Assets for Robotics with Frontier AI Models,” October 8, 2026, 00:20–00:50. https://www.youtube.com/shorts/H5UaOdIZERw

## Scene 4 — PLM is the higher-stakes boundary

**Narration:** Autodesk executive Jeff Kinder says an agentic PLM experience is expected to launch in the next couple of quarters. He says the proposed experience would draw information from Slack, Microsoft Teams, and Outlook conversations. And it could discern PLM information in those unstructured conversations and place it into the PLM system.

This is a source claim about a planned capability, not deployed behavior. The consequential check is not just whether extraction sounds plausible. It is whether extraction, attribution, revision context, approval context, and entry into PLM are correct and auditable.

**Visual:** Conversation bubbles from Slack, Teams, and Outlook flow to an “AI inference” box, then stop at a large “Human approval + audit trail” gate before “PLM system of record.”

## Scene 5 — The adoption rule

**Narration:** My interpretation is to sequence adoption by reversibility and consequence. Start with discovery, where a person can inspect the result before acting. Move to simulation preparation when the team can retain the input references, asset changes, check results, and simulation conditions. Before an agent writes inferred information into PLM, require an auditable approval boundary.

This is not a claim that one system is inherently more accurate. It follows from what each workflow says it does: Onshape limits this released feature to public-document retrieval, while Autodesk describes a future workflow that would place inferred information into PLM.

**Visual:** Comparison table. Rows: Discovery / Simulation preparation / PLM writing. Columns: “Observed check,” “Consequence of error,” and “Required control.” PLM writing shows “Auditable approval required.”

**Source on screen:** 03-c13 — Inference from Onshape, October 5, 2026, and Engineering.com, September 22, 2026.

## Scene 7 — What to do next

**Narration:** Run a narrow retrieval pilot with human inspection. For simulation workflows, keep the references, asset changes, check results, and simulation conditions. For any proposed PLM automation, require approval before inferred content becomes a record.

Watch next for published deployment evidence, explicit review controls, and physical-task results that extend beyond simulation. That is a corporate-development claim, not evidence of a capability outcome.
