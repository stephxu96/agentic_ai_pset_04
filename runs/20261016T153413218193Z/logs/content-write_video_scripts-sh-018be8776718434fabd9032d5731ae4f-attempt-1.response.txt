Adopt the workflow, not the unchecked output. Use AI for first drafts, reusable feature development, model search, and simulation preparation. A capability claim holds only as far as the check that observed it. A valid solid does not prove a part is manufacturable. A good-looking model does not prove its dimensions are right. And a completed simulation workflow does not prove physical transfer.\n\n**Visual:** Three paired cards: “Valid model ≠ manufacturable part,” “Mass check ≠ full drawing conformance,” and “Simulation-ready ≠ manufacturing-release ready.”\n\n**Source on screen:** 03-c1 — MIT Computational Design and Fabrication Group, “On the Opportunities and Risks of Frontier Models for 3D Modeling, Computational Design and Robotics,” September 22, 2026.\n\n## Scene 3 — What the survey does and does not show\n\n**Narration:** MIT’s survey reports that its record does not show frontier-model outputs meeting engineering requirements such as tolerances or manufacturability. It does not prove that no model can meet those requirements.\n\nThe survey identifies the missing check: tolerance specifications and manufacturability have not been tested on physical parts in the CAD evidence it reviewed.\n\n**Visual:** A checklist labeled “Observed in the record”: editable CAD. Then define explicit release gates. Check body count and topology for assemblies. Check dimensions and tolerances for parts. Check process-specific manufacturability for production. Use representative physical tests where hardware behavior matters.\n\nWatch next for disclosed, representative evaluations. They should separate model output from the CAD, simulation, and review systems around it. They should state what was checked, on what inputs, and where the system failed.\n\n**Visual:** Closing checklist: “Use workflow automation” and “Match every release claim to its check.” End card: “Watch for disclosed evaluations with representative inputs and failure reporting.”\n\n**Source on screen:** 03-c1, 03-c2, 03-c3, 03-c8 — MIT Computational Design and Fabrication Group, September 22, 2026; Engineering.com, September 30, 2026.\n\n## Video description\n\nCompanion article: [ARTICLE LINK]\n\nReferences:\n\n- MIT Computational Design and Fabrication Group, “On the Opportunities and Risks of Frontier Models for 3D Modeling, Computational Design and Robotics,” September 22, 2026. https://mit-cdfg.github.io/Survey-AI-for-3D-modeling-Robotics/\n- Engineering.com, “Welcome to the vibe-CAD era,” September 30, 2026. https://www.engineering.com/welcome-to-the-vibe-cad-era/\n- Onshape, “5 Ways Teams Can Leverage the Onshape FeatureScript MCP Server,” September 30, 2026. https://www.onshape.com/en/blog/featurescript-mcp-server-use-cases-text-code-cad\n- NVIDIA, “5 Steps to Create SimReady Assets for Robotics with Frontier AI Models,” October 8, 2026, 0:20–0:28 and 0:37–0:50. https://www.youtube.com/shorts/H5UaOdIZERw\n- Physical AI Podcast, “AI Models Fail 63 of 84 Robot Tasks, Waymo Borrows $5B,” October 10, 2026, 0:28–0:34 and 2:23–2:27. https://dts.podtrac.com/redirect.mp3/op3.dev/e,pg=83ccf97e-1d88-565b-ad28-0411c3a101af/physicalaipodcast.co/audio/episode-010.mp3"}
